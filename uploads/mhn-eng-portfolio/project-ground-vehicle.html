Ground Vehicle Aerodynamic Benchmark

High-speed CFD study of a smooth, streamlined bluff body in close ground proximity, benchmarked against published minimum-drag references and pushed further with adjoint-based shape optimization.

Inlet speed
300 km/h
Turbulence model
k-ω SST
Mesh
Polyhedral, ground proximity
Solver
Ansys Fluent

Approach

A smooth, streamlined bluff body was simulated in close ground proximity at 300 km/h inlet velocity in Ansys Fluent, using a k-ω SST turbulence closure and a polyhedral mesh refined around the body surface and the ground-clearance gap, where the flow is most sensitive to mesh quality.

Rather than treat the result in isolation, I benchmarked it against three published references with well-characterized aerodynamic behaviour: the Morelli minimum-drag body, the DrivAer generic sedan geometry, and simplified teardrop analogs — giving the result a defensible position against known low-drag and automotive-realistic baselines rather than an unanchored number.

Going further: adjoint optimization

To explore how much more performance was available, I ran an adjoint-based shape optimization study in Fluent targeting zero lift, using free-form deformation (FFD) parameterization of the surface. Adjoint methods compute the sensitivity of an objective (here, lift) to every surface mesh point in a single additional solve, making them far cheaper than parametric sweeps for high-dimensional shape studies.
